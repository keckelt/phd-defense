Correction alone is not the answer

Sample or population? Looking at data for the first time, people describe the sample in front of them. Scoring that against the population may measure a misunderstanding, not a false insight.

Insight or action? Analysis usually serves a decision, and the decision has to be made anyway. True or false labels ignore what acting on an insight costs and gains.

A blank slate? Significance testing has no place for what the analyst already knows. A Bayesian view starts from priors, and experts who see the same kind of data again and again have them.

What this means for my approaches

  • Correct the matrix. Its parallel tests are countable, so their p-values can be adjusted, with Holm or Benjamini-Hochberg. That is planned for TourDino.
  • Confirm on new data. Held-out data gave the fewest false discoveries in Zgraggen et al.
  • Show uncertainty in the charts. It led to fewer false discoveries than no correction (Sarma et al.).
  • Part of the record exists. Coral's evolution graph and Loops' history keep what was created and run, a start for counting the looks. Using it for correction, for example with alpha investing (Zhao et al.), is future work.
  • Show uncertainty by default. Hullman and Heer suggest it. Coral's Prevalence operation shows a 95% confidence interval.
  • Guidance in complex systems. One of the grand challenges of visual analytics applications (Wu et al. 2023).
