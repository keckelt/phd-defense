The Kokiri workflow

The overlap view

  • It sits at the top of Kokiri, because overlapping cohorts are harder to tell apart, and the random forest may assign a shared item to more than one cohort.
  • Chosen over UpSet, the Jaccard similarity, and plain counts of shared items: it is compact and shows the relative sizes of the cohorts.
  • In the case study: the Black cohort is a subset of the whole NSCLC cohort, and much smaller, with an overlap of 6.9%.

Gini importance is biased, and it is still the right choice

  • True: Gini importance favors attributes with many levels and correlated attributes. Strobl et al. 2007, cited in the chapter.
  • It is already computed while the forest is built, so it is free. That is what makes the incremental, human-in-the-loop retraining possible.
  • The ranking is a starting point for exploration, not a claim.
  • FGFR4 is the proof: a highly ranked attribute that was an artifact, caught because the user could open the distribution behind the rank.
  • My rewrite of Coral reduces the bias: trees draw without replacement, as Strobl et al. propose, and each attribute is judged against a shuffled copy of itself.

What Kokiri's accuracy does and does not say

  • The 74.3% is measured on the same patients the forest was trained on. It is optimistic, an upper bound.
  • The out-of-bag score, on the patients each tree did not see, is computed but not shown. My rewrite of Coral shows it instead.
  • Even as an upper bound, 74.3% says the six cohorts overlap a lot. Most Asian and Black patients were assigned to the two large White cohorts, despite balanced class weights.
  • The number describes how separable these cohorts are. It is not a model to classify new patients, and the thesis does not use it as one.
  • The per-patient probabilities come from the same forest. The supervised UMAP of them pulls cohorts apart on purpose, so misclassified patients stand out. It is a way to find them, not evidence that the cohorts separate.

Other ways to explain the forest

What it says Cost Fits the step-by-step training?
Gini importance How much each attribute helps to split the cohorts. Biased toward many levels. Free, computed while the forest grows Yes: a new ranking every 25 trees
Permutation importance How much accuracy drops when one attribute is shuffled. Less biased. One extra pass per attribute; for 719 genes, slow Only at the end, or on a sample
SHAP values How much each attribute pushes each single patient toward a cohort High for large forests and many patients Fits the per-patient view, not the ranking loop
Rules, subgroup discovery Which combination separates the cohorts: "ATM and NF1 both mutated" instead of two separate ranks Rule extraction or a separate search A next step: explain the ranking with rules
Shadow features Is an attribute more important than a shuffled copy of itself? Confirmed, tentative or rejected. Twice the columns: slower on small data, fewer rows on large Yes: the verdict firms up as trees are added. Built in my rewrite of Coral.
