Future work: groups without crisp boundaries

  • Every group in this thesis has a crisp boundary: a patient is in or out, whether the group comes from a filter in Coral or from a cluster in the embedding.
  • Soft or fuzzy clustering allows a continuum, along which items move from one group to another (Zadeh 1965).
  • The hard part is showing it: uncertainty in set membership, beyond a yes or no (Tominski et al. 2023).

Zadeh. Fuzzy sets. Information and Control, 1965. Tominski, Behrisch, Bleisch, Fabrikant, Mayr, Miksch, Purchase. Visualizing uncertainty in sets. IEEE CG&A 2023.

Soft groups: where my approaches already point that way

  • Kokiri already gives degrees. Every patient gets a probability for every cohort. Patients with no clear cohort are the soft cases, and Kokiri shows them.
  • The embedding uses HDBSCAN. Its automatic groups come from density-based clustering, which can also give a membership strength per point. The contours on the projection already show density fading out.
  • Coral's filters are crisp. A patient is in or out. Soft cohorts would need weighted members, and charts that show the weights.
  • Tests and summaries would change. TourDino's tests and the summary and difference views count each patient once, in one group. With weights, both would have to count partial members.

McInnes, Healy, Astels. hdbscan: hierarchical density based clustering. JOSS 2017. Tominski, Behrisch, Bleisch, Fabrikant, Mayr, Miksch, Purchase. Visualizing uncertainty in sets. IEEE CG&A 2023.

Future work: AI as an analysis partner

  • This thesis uses machine learning as a tool: a random forest in Kokiri, dimensionality reduction for the embeddings.
  • AI in the loop: agents take over tasks, while responsibility and control stay with the human expert (Rogers et al. 2024, Bühler et al. 2025).
  • Agentic visualization: a forager agent searches for data, an analyst agent suggests where to look next (Dhanoa et al. 2025).
  • The new tracking problem: which step was done by whom, human or AI. Loops already shows which analyst ran what; AI agents as contributors are the natural next step.

Rogers et al. Visualization and automation in data science. IEEE VDS 2024. Bühler, Höllt, Schultz, Vázquez. AI-in-the-loop. IEEE CG&A 2025. Dhanoa, Wolter, León, Schulz, Elmqvist. Agentic visualization. IEEE CG&A 2025.

Agents in the sensemaking loop

The agent The human Where it touches my approaches
Foraging A forager agent searches for relevant data and prepares it, the tedious part users want automated. Decides which data and which groups matter. Coral's filters and splits, driven by natural language.
Sensemaking An analyst agent suggests promising directions to explore. Builds the case, tests hypotheses, presents the result. Kokiri's ranking as a source of suggestions; TourDino to check them.
Provenance Every step it takes has to be traceable. Needs to know what the agent did, and when. Loops already records which contributor ran each cell: agents become contributors.

Future work: the grand challenges of visual analytics

  • Closer intertwining of VA and AI: the human and AI partnership from the previous slide.
  • Providing guidance in complex VA systems: correcting for multiple comparisons, so users explore more reliably.
  • Expanding the domain space and user groups: all approaches of this thesis are open source, and making them accessible is the next step.

Wu, Deng, Chen, Liu, Keim, Maciejewski, Miksch, Strobelt, Viégas, Wattenberg. Grand challenges in visual analytics applications. IEEE CG&A 2023.
